### Question 
Which of the following is a valid way to label a sample mean? Select all that apply.

*  $\sigma$
*  $\bar{x}$
*  $\bar{y}$
*  $\hat{x}$
*  $\mu$

### Correct Answer
FALSE
TRUE
TRUE
FALSE
FALSE

### Solution:
The correct answers are **$\bar{y}$** and **$\bar{x}$**.

Sample means are denoted using **bar notation** (e.g., $\bar{x}$ or $\bar{y}$), where the bar over the variable indicates the mean of the sample data.

**Why the other options are incorrect:**

* **$\mu$** (mu) is the symbol for the **population mean**, not the sample mean. Greek letters are reserved for population parameters.

* **$\hat{x}$** (x-hat) typically represents a **predicted or fitted value** in regression analysis, not a sample mean. While both are estimates, they serve different purposes.

* **$\sigma$** (sigma) is the symbol for the **population standard deviation**, not a measure of central tendency.

**Key distinction:** Sample statistics use Roman letters with special notation (like the bar), while population parameters use Greek letters.
